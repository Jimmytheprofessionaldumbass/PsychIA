\section{Introduction}

    
In an increasingly digital society, many teenagers and young adults find issues struggling to focus academically as distractions become ever more prevalent, many new terms such as ``flow state'' and the state of being ``locked in'' having increasingly used in social media platforms describe ``flow'', or, the mental state of total deep engrossment/absorption in a cognitive work and an ensuing loss of self-consciousness.\parencite{Mazid2024} This state is linked to an optimal improvement/learning schedule\parencite{Mazid2024}. %better word it?
And hence, in the contemporary world, this is a particularly an important field of research as our target population of teenagers, given too many choices in life, would find it easier to engage in activities such as watching television, ``doomscrolling'', and gaming due to the lack of pressure for teenagers to doing ``challenging activities that can teach and improve new skills'' \parencite{Schwartz2000,Mazid2024}. In this stage in life according to Piaget's theory of development teenagers are transitioning into higher-order thinking and requires a lot of heavy learning to occur, hence, it is important for them to engage in skill learning as it is a critical developmental stage in human life. 
``Being in the state of flow meant having an optimal experience which has been referred to as ‘the bottom line of existence (because) without it there would be little purpose in living.’'' \parencite{Mazid2024}


Modern research has identified 9 characteristics of the flow experience. The challenge skill balance is often considered the backbone of flow research. This characteristic suggests that flow is reached when the perceived challenge of the task matches the skill level of the participant. Illustrated in figure \ref{fig:challenge-skill}. This parallels to Vygotsky's ``Zone of Proximal Development'' which is being in a skill level where it requires help from a more knowledgeable other rather than being impossible nor able to be done without any external assistance.
\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \center
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{challenge skill balance}
    \caption{The challenge-skill balance model of flow state \parencite{Mazid2024}}
    \label{fig:challenge-skill}
\end{figure}

However, many other factors were later discovered in relating to flow, such as perceived control. However, this does not necessarily mean advancement in resources. For instance, giving one a calculator in a math test, may increase their ``perceived control'', but that is because it does really increase their control in their situation, similar to having access to more money/funding to accomplish a task. And it is imperative that we operationalize perceived control carefully. 
In \parencite{AINLEY2008109} it is operationalized as the opportunity to choose the topic they wish to engage a writing task on which is relevant to our target population because students of the DP program are required to write many research articles such as Internal Assessments, Extended essays, etc and often struggle with choosing a topic. In our research study, perceived control will refer to whether the participants are able to choose their topic for a writing task.

Hence, this research will focus on the effect of perceived control on the magnitude of flow measured as a likert scale based on absorption, timelessness and effort. %Somehow bold this

\subsection{Research Method Choice}

We will be doing a true experiment because it isolates the antecedent and consequent of behaviour, preparing us a good opportunity to find a causal relationship. Namely, the effect of perceived control on flow state.

Students will be asked to write a short two paragrpah essay on a computer program under three different experimental conditions. The lowest being directly given a research question, the second having the ability to choose a research question from three research questions, and the last one being to come up with any research question they would be interested in. The computer program will present one perceived control condition randomly to the participants. Three existing research questions exist and will be presented according to their conditions (e.g. if they are low perceived control, they are randomly selected a research question, if they are medium perceived control, they are given all three questions). The three research questions are "To what extent does governmental policy affect climate change?", "What renewable energy source appears most practical in your country" and "how can negative impacts of social media be minimized for teenagers". In the case of open ended prompt, the computer will present "You are given the opportunity to come up with a research question in the context of whatever topic you choose".

\subsection{Designing the Data Collection}


Many methods of data collection for flow has been used such as the four-channel model ratio of challenge and skill, and a classification based on ratings of absorption, timelessness and effort and as stated by ainley: "Sometimes flow is defined in terms of the balance between challenge and skill. At other times, on-task experiences of absorption, a sense of timelessness, and a feeling that the activity was effortless are used as indicators of flow." \parencite{AINLEY2008109}. Ainley uses 9 question items, however, we will focus on using a classification based on absorption, timelessness and effort. Since this better describes the positive and desirable effects of flow we are interested in optimizing rather than the theoretical preconditions that affect flow such as skill-challenge balance. We will in addition include perceived control and difficulty to be able to identify correlations between flow and sense of control because by knowing the relationship between control and difficulty, we can have a better understanding of whether it is truly perceived control or their advanced knowledge in the field that resulted in flow.

Before they start they will be suggested to "work as you normally would" rather than "try to finish the task" to reduce pressure on the assignment before working moving onto the first section of the study. We will collect the data regarding absorption, timelessness, etc using probes in the form of questionnaires throughout the research session (the full detail is provided in the appendix). The wording was chosen carefully to sound neutral to reduce anchoring bias.

% The questionnaire we will be using to measure flow state is the Absorption–timelessness–effort classification based on \parencite{AINLEY2008109} which is a 5 point likert questionaire (described in detail in the Appendix). Ainley used a total of 9 question items,

    \item When the average typing speed over a span of 2 minutes is greater than 50wpm

\end{enumerate} 

A modified version of a computer program "Between the lines" \parencite{Ainley2002} will be used to prompt these questions according to these rules.

At the end they will be rewarded for their efforts, as well as a debriefing screen (shown in the appendix).

\subsection{Potential Challenges}

The typing speed probe is limited by their approach to writing. The idea is based on the fact that, when people are in flow, they are likely developing a lot of ideas and writing a lot of their ideas down. But 50wpm was chosen as a minimum, most students in our school type around 100, so it is also possible for the probe to fire every 2 minutes, disturbing their flow state. Which could lead to higher probing rate and can affect the accuracy, they could just fake the answers just to focus on the task or quickly capture a new idea they had rather than sincerely answering the probe. One solution is to have the probe be dependent on time instead of conditions. However that also has conditions as the way different people work is more different and different times could result in completely different parts of their flow and is equivalent to randomly selecting time. Another solution is to seperate the essay into seperate sections so we can categorically measure their flow state and investigation variables.

Another challenge is the link between familiarity within their topic and ability to experience flow; since they are already confortable with their topic, they could be already very confortable with writing about it, making it difficult to determine whether it is truly perceived control that influences their flow or other factors, since we could be influencing other factors like their knowledge of a particular topic. If the independent variable becomes the amount of research questions they could choose, it could also involve paradox of choice, which suggests control isn't directly proportional to the number of choices; more choices could lead to dissatisfaction and lower levels of control.
